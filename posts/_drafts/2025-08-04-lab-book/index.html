Introduction

What do you expect this blog post to be about? Seriously, take 10 seconds (no more) and make a guess.

Making predictions about new information before being formally exposed to it can prime one to make deeper connections with one’s pre-existing web of knowledge.1 This allows one to solidify old neural pathways while generating new ones, strengething one’s immediate understanding of the knowledge as well. Later on, that connection (and the information itself) is solidified through the practice of retrieving the knowledge. This naturally separates learning into three-acts: thinking before one learns something new, during the learning process, and after the learning has been “complete”.2

The Purpose of the Lab Book

This three-act structure may be familiar, for example taking the form of lab reports, where you have a hypothesis, the experiment itself, and the results/discussion. Teaching, learning, and professional development are, in many ways, a continual experiement where none of the variables are held constant. I plan to implement a similar structure for presenting, tracking, and reviewing my professorial career.

This blog, then, can be considered my professional development lab book. In it, I intend to express my expectations, my decisions, and my reactions/reflections throughout my professorial career.3

This Post as a Preface

If this blog is a lab book, this post is the introduction to my first attempt at keeping a lab book. In it, I intend to set the stage for how I expect to maintain this lab book throughout the upcoming term, and (perhaps) beyond. This is a meta-post, in the sense that it is itself a preview of my experimental method; later, I should end up making some meta-notes about the experiment of this experimental method, and after that, review some of the things that wend well and what went poorly, and how to revise my method.4

The Experiment

This upcoming term, I plan to implement three phases to the experiment of my professorship. I will call these three stages the PREviews, the INTERviews, and the REviews of the term.5

In the rest of this section, I outline some of my current working philosophy behind the three-act structure (PRE-, INTER-, and RE-views) I expect my notes to take.

PREviews

WarningSummary

Influenced by past REviews, and aimed at future INTERviews, PREview posts will break down some of my current nebulous ideas, plans, and goals into goals, plans, and ideas worth investigating.

PREviews will accompany the start of a new, nebulous or otherwise large-scale agenda (e.g., new semester, new research project, new professional development goal) for which there is no due date, or where the due date is very far away. Due dates for regular REviews should be proposed. These posts will break down large-scale tasks into somewhat smaller-scope tasks/goals (which may themselves be nebulous in scope). I will present my current understanding/ideas of this larger task, as well as make predictions about how I might grow or develop (or not!). This will give me some frame of reference for how my own thinking has changed (or not!) when I later revisit these goals.9

NoteExample

I want to attain promotion to associate professor of mathematics by spring 2031. This is a large-scale, personal goal, which has a nebulous agenda. I may write a PREview blog post about this goal, outlining some of the specific institutional expectations, as well as ways I hope to meet those expectations. Because each step/expectation will itself be somewhat nebulous, each step (such as demonstrating “evidence of continued growth towards teaching excellence”) would likely require its own PREview blog post, where I track some of my expectations/initial thoughts about it, as well as how I plan to learn more about it, etc.

INTERviews

WarningSummary

Influenced by past PREviews, aimed at future REviews, I plan to conduct INTERviews as a way of touching base on the core elements of a larger project.

INTERviews will accompany the (in)completion of small-scope, specific events which were outlined as a part of a PREview (e.g., read a book, attend a specific workshop, test out a particular pedagogy practice) for which there is a due date proposed for completion. These posts will be used to summarize the task and how it went (e.g., a book review) from a neutral perspective, coupled with my somewhat-immediate reaction to it, therefore serving as an interview both for the material and for myself in response to the material.10

NoteExample

I want to learn about “alternative grading” methods. Because this step is itself nebulous, it might have a task “attend the MathFest25 minicourse on implementing alternative grading on 2025-08-08”. Because this workshop has a definite event associated to it, I would likely write a blog post explaining the core knowledge I gained from the workshop, as well as how I responded to it, what I found useful, and any further events I would like to participate in.

REviews

WarningSummary

Influenced by past INTERviews, aimed at informing future PREviews, I plan to conduct REviews as a systematic synthesis of my growth and progress through large-scale/nebulous development.

REviews will be used to assess my progress towards large-scare goals/projects, often as an attempt to synthesize and connect some of the relevant material. These posts should therefore be linked to due dates proposed by PREviews, while reflecting some of the knowledge and understanding developed throughout the relevant INTERviews. These REviews may therefore look more like philosophizing, proposing/testing my ideas and beliefs, and asking questions for further exploration. (Note: these will not be works of publishable quality, nor should they be treated as such.)

NoteExample

Remember that I want to learn about “alternative grading” methods. Assume I’ve learned about them throughout the course of a year, and recall that I have (supposedly) attended a workshop at MathFest25 on the topic. Assume I’ve also read through three different syllabi that utilize alternative grading. At the end of that year, I might write a review about the pros/cons of alternative grading, and my response to it; at the end of that response, I may conclude that I want to attempt an implementation of alternative grading. This would likely lead me to create a new PREview post where I discuss my plan to implement alternative grading.

Conclusion

I’m excited about this. I have to say, I came back to this about two days after “uploading” it for the first time, and realized that I had a lot more crystalized ideas than I originally had. I hope that I can learn from that.11

I hope that this PRE-, INTER-, and RE-view structure allows me to easily jump into writing blog posts in brief regular sessions, and allows me to naturally develop a portfolio of my professional development moving forward. Note that this means there likely won’t be “regular” updates, because not everything happens on a regular schedule. This is great! That actually gives me the freedom to self-impose due dates, to try my best to keep those dates, and to develop and refine my structure without being locked into a rhythm that isn’t going to work for me. I expect my next blog post will be about some of my teaching-specific goals for this upcoming year, but who knows? I guess time (and this website) will tell.
