In this “preview,” I specify my professional goals as well as an outline for how I hope to reach these goals. As a new professor, my professional goals are perhaps predictable. This blog is aimed at helping me effectively track my expectations and progress towards these goals, while providing a public space for me to share the ideas that I encounter along the way.

Goals

  1. Attain promotion to associate professor at Westmont College by Spring 2031
  2. Attain recommendation for tenure at Westmont College by Spring 2031

Like I said, predictable.1

Plans

First, I want to check and see what the faculty handbook specified about promotion/tenure. After reviewing the specifications outlined there, I will attempt to crystalize some long-term strategies that will help me attain those specifications.

So here’s task number one (which I’ve already done!):

I guess that means I get to do a little “interview” of the handbook

Interviewing the Faculty Handbook

The faculty handbook has four main sections that I’d like to report on.

  1. Standards
  2. Criteria
  3. Evidence
  4. Procedure

I’m going to work my way backwards, starting with the procedure and ending with the standards. The “procedure” is identical for both promotion and tenure, detailing the deliverables that I (and the reviewers) will be assessing; naturally, these deliverables should include some evidence that I have met the criteria for promotion/tenure, and

The tenure review process includes an “intermediate review” whose purpose is “to make a judgment concerning the reviewee’s progress towards tenure and to make a judgment as to whether or not the candidate is on a trajectory that would lead to tenure.” Naturally, I will want to make sure I hit some of my benchmarks before this review. Therefore I’m going to try to make significant progress on each goal before Spring 2028.

Procedure

According to the handbook, at the time of review, I will be responsible for submitting the following materials in the form of a portfolio:

  1. a current curriculum vitae;
  2. personal statement (self-assessment essay and faith-learning portfolio as described in section 2.2.1.3 e))
  3. course evaluations for all courses taught during the previous two years, or the results of alternative means of evaluation previously approved by the and the Personnel Committee;
  4. the schedule and class rosters for each current course;
  5. a list of students for potential interviews;
  6. a list of faculty to be contacted;
  7. a list of external references to be contacted (required of candidates for promotion, optional in other reviews).

Items 1, 3, and 4 are not items I cannot really prepare for in advance. Items 5, 6, and 7 can be curated by careful consideration and evaluation during each semester. Item 2 is discussed in more detail in Evidence below.

Plan: References

By the end of each term (Fall, Spring, Summer), record for yourself an interaction/experience with

  1. one student
  2. one faculty
  3. one external reference

from that term exemplifies (or fails to exemplify) one or more of the standards described below. These interactions will not be shared publicly without prior consent.

Evidence

According to the handbook, promotion and tenure decisions will be based on the following evidence:

  1. student evaluations
  2. peer evaluations
  3. departmental evaluations
  4. administrative evaluations
  5. candidate’s personal statement (self-assessment and faith-learning portfolio) and curriculum vitae
  1. Self-assessment The 5-10 page document addresses the standards of faculty review in the Faculty Handbook, including the fundamental criterion described under 2.2.1. It presents the candidate’s own assessment of his or her development and accomplishments in the areas of teaching, professional development, and institutional service, and describes the ways in which the candidate would like to grow in the future.
  2. Faith-Learning Portfolio This collection is both a record for use in reviewing the candidate and a tool for use by the faculty member in seeking to develop his or her own approach to the integration of faith and learning. Considered a work in progress, it may include reading lists of relevant material, reflections on those readings and/or on the faculty member’s other efforts to integrate faith and learning in the classroom, in scholarship, and in participation in the life of the community. The portfolio should include an essay reflecting the candidate’s current thinking about the philosophy of Christian liberal arts education and about the relationship between the candidate’s Christian faith and academic discipline. At the time of final tenure review, the essay should have evolved into a 2,500-5,000 word paper on these topics.

The first four of these deliverables are, in many ways, outside of my control except in how I demonstrate the Criteria (see below) on a daily basis. Since the self-assessment document is meant to be provided as evidence of my development and accomplishments in various areas, it will help me to keep a written record of this, as well as to continually be assessing areas of growth.

Plan: Self-Assessment

By the start of each term, for each of the following critera categories

  1. Foundational Criteria,
  2. Teaching,
  3. Professional Development, and
  4. Institutional Service

select two separate standards you would like to grow/develop in, and outline your plan for development in those standards.

By the end of each term, write at least one reflection (500 words or less) correlating to each of the standards previously selected in which you detail a specific event that demonstrates your success or failure in meeting a given standard.

Furthermore, for each of the broader criteria categories describe one event that indicates a necessary area for continued growth, tied to a specific standard (again 500 words or less).2

The Faith-Learning Portfolio has a few suggested deliverables; I think that this blog will actually be a valuable way of building up my portfolio and allowing me to curate my reading materials, some of my “best” reflections, and will give me a space to identify some faith-learning practices. I also expect this blog can give me an outlet for refining my philosophy of Christian liberal arts education and the connection between my faith and mathematics.
